# Manchester United Financial Analysis | 2021–2025

![Manchester United Financial Analysis Dashboard](Manchester%20United%20Financial%20Analysis%20Dashboard.jpg)

## Project Overview

I built this project to analyse how Manchester United's financial performance has changed over the last five years, while developing my Excel and financial analysis skills.

As a Manchester United supporter, I wanted to work with a business I already follow closely rather than use a generic dataset. Using the club's published financial information, I looked at how its revenue has developed, whether that growth has translated into profitability, and how its debt position has changed between 2021 and 2025.

I then brought the analysis together into an Excel dashboard to make the main trends and findings easy to interpret.

## What I Analysed

The workbook looks at three main areas of Manchester United's financial performance:

### Revenue
I analysed total revenue alongside the club's three main revenue streams — commercial, broadcasting and matchday revenue — and calculated year-on-year revenue growth.

### Profitability
I compared revenue with operating expenses, operating profit/loss and net profit/loss to see whether the club's revenue growth has translated into improved profitability.

### Debt and Financial Position
I analysed cash, borrowings, net debt, total assets and liabilities, as well as the debt-to-assets ratio, to understand how the club's financial position has changed over the period.

## Key Findings

**Revenue has grown strongly.**  
Manchester United's revenue increased from £494.1m in 2021 to £666.5m in 2025 — an increase of approximately 35%.

**Higher revenue has not yet produced consistent profitability.**  
Despite the growth in revenue, the club recorded a net loss in every year analysed. However, the net loss improved considerably from £113.2m in 2024 to £33.0m in 2025.

**Debt remains significant.**  
Net debt increased from approximately £419.5m in 2021 to £550.9m in 2025. However, borrowings represented 38.9% of total assets in 2025, the lowest proportion across the five-year period.

## My Takeaway

The part I found most interesting was that revenue growth alone does not tell you whether a business is performing well financially.

Manchester United generated substantially more revenue in 2025 than in 2021, but that growth did not translate into consistent profits. Looking at profitability and debt alongside revenue gave me a much better understanding of the club's overall financial position than looking at the headline revenue figure on its own.

That was one of the main things I wanted to learn from this project: how the different parts of a company's financial statements work together to tell a wider commercial story.
